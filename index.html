    How grading works

    Each step is graded against its own standard: the step's optimum, or for set-up steps the best total with the step that follows. Meeting it or beating it is a pass. Spaced repetition: a case you miss comes back after 3 solves and leaves the pool once you meet the standard 4 times in a row (after 3, 8 and 25 more solves, then after an 8-hour break). Another miss restarts the schedule.

    Three facts this settles

    • Clean fringe lands around 100–120 at speed. You were right: the leaderboard median ao100 is 104 moves.
    • The best speed averages are around 78, not under 70. The top 10 ao100s run 75–94 moves at 21–29 TPS. Sub-70 shows up in their best ao12s (64.7–69.2). It also shows up in slow, efficiency-only averages: ao100 records of 55.9 to 66.7 moves at 1–6 TPS. Speed and movecount trade off at the top.
    • Sub-70 at speed means beating stage-optimal play. Perfect stage-by-stage fringe is about 70. Getting under it consistently takes planning across stages: blocks that span the row and column, and choosing which stage to finish first.
    • The TPS ceiling is far higher than most guides say. The best ao100s are at 28–31 TPS; ao12 34; 3x3 ao100 37.

    Stage 1 · last six cells (L6P)

    3x2

    360 positions. Optimal average 12.62; the hardest needs 21 (one position). Tiles 10, 11, 12, 14, 15.

    The method costs almost nothing. Solve 10 over 14 as a column pair, then cycle the last three. Done with the best choices, that averages 12.78, only 0.16 over optimal. So the moves you lose here don't come from the method. They come from building the 10/14 pair the long way, or from cycling the 2x2 the wrong direction.

    What to actually learn

    • The six cells are a ring. Running the gap around the ring rotates all five tiles one step. Most solutions are one rotation direction plus at most one pass through the middle edge.
    • Pair 10/14 in the shortest way. Either build the pair as a vertical domino and drop it in, or use the hook: park 14 in 10's cell with 10 beside it, then rotate.
    • 2x2 finish: 12 positions, max 6 moves, mean 3. Read the cycle direction before you move.
    • Force easy endings early. While you insert 10 and 14, steer 11, 12, 15 into cyclic order so the last step is a pure rotation (advice from the old Speedsolving thread).

    Drill: scramble, solve, compare to optimal. Average within 0.5 of optimal, then make it a burst well above your average TPS. Top players run endings at 30+ TPS.

    Stage 2 · the 8 puzzle

    3x3

    181,440 positions, optimal average 21.97, hardest 31. Tiles 6–8, 10–12, 14–15. Leaderboard top-10 3x3 ao100s average 23–28 moves at 25–37 TPS. The leaderboard median is 32.7.

    Where moves go (exact)

    • Always solve row 6-7-8 by the shortest route, then the 3x2: 27.3 if you ignore what the 3x2 will look like.
    • Among the shortest row solutions, pick the one that leaves the easiest 3x2: 25.8 (saves 1.4).
    • Also choose between row 6-7-8 and column 6-10-14, whichever is cheaper: 23.9 (saves another 1.9).
    • Fully optimal, allowed to break and rebuild: 21.97.

    So row-or-column choice is the single biggest learnable gain on the 3x3. The trainer's 3x3 methods follow this ladder: row or column then the 3x2, the same set up for the 3x2, "row or column, whichever is better", an opening drill, and full optimal. When you miss, the replay shows the exact move where the standard parts from yours.

    The last-two trick

    For 7 and 8 (the corner pair), set 8 in 7's cell with 7 directly below, then rotate both in. The column version (10 over 14) is the mirror. Better still, avoid needing it: place 7 and 8 as a horizontal pair and slide them in together.

    Stage 3 · after row 1

    4x3

    239.5 million positions, too many to learn whole, so the 4x3 trainer trains the column step: 11,880 cases for 5-9-13, graded exactly, with the other tiles greyed out. The set-up methods grade the column together with the optimal 3x3 it leaves.

    Column (fringe) or row (LBL)

    Column 5-9-13 costs 18.8 optimal on average and leaves a 3x3 (about 24 with good row-or-column choice). Row 5–8 costs 21.1 and leaves a 4x2 (22.7 optimal). The totals are nearly identical (≈43–45), which is why top players report fringe and LBL as about equal and many use both. Choose by the scramble, not by habit.

    9 and 13

    Pair them before you insert: 9 on top of 13 as a vertical domino, then send them up together. The classic waste is placing 9, then breaking it to fetch 13.

    Overlap into the 3x3

    While inserting the column, carry 6 (or 6 and 10) along when they are on the way. Stage-perfect play is about 70; getting under 70 at speed needs exactly this kind of overlap.

    Measured: an optimal column followed by an optimal 3x3 averages 40.8 moves when the column ignores the 3x3, and 39.4 when it picks the optimal column that leaves the best 3x3. Letting the column spend extra moves to force a better 3x3 pays: up to +2 moves gives 37.7, up to +4 gives 36.6, up to +6 gives 35.9. The "up to +2" method trains the first of these.

    Stage 4 · the full 15 puzzle

    4x4

    About 10.5 trillion positions; optimal average 52.6; hardest 80 (43 counting a whole-line slide as one move).

    Row 1 decides the solve

    The optimal row 1 already costs 26.9 on average, the largest single stage. Tiles 1 and 2 alone average 16.3 optimal.

    • Plan 1 and 2 completely before the first move. Most sims start the clock on your first move, so planning time before it is free.
    • Place 3 and 4 as a vertical pair and insert them in one rotation.
    • Choose row first or column first (1-5-9-13) by which tiles are closer. The column-first stages have the same budgets by symmetry.
    • End row 1 with the gap where the next stage needs it.

    The 4x4 trainer trains the first block only, by the method you choose: one tile at a time (about 38.5 moves for row 1), two pairs (35.5), the whole row as one step (26.9), the row set up for column 5-9-13, or column-first and L-shaped orders. Splitting row 1 into pairs costs about 8 moves, so the whole-row method is the target.

    Hover mode in the trainer imitates Slidysim's mouse hover control: the gap follows your pointer along its row or column. Full 3x3 solves can feed the advisor (Settings → Use in advisor).

    Every technique I could find

    Collected from the Speedsolving thread, the Slidysim README, the leaderboard data, and the exact computations above. A lot of current know-how lives in the Speedsliding Discord and in replays rather than in written guides. Where a technique's value can be measured, the number is given.

    Solution finding

    Reduction (fringe)method
    Row 1 + column 5-9-13, then row 6–8 + column 10-14, then cycle the 2x2. The standard speed method, and it scales to big puzzles. Within each L, do row first, column first, or both at once.Speedsolving 15-puzzle thread
    Layer by layermethod
    Row 1, row 2, then the 4x2 column by column (9&13, 10&14, cycle 11-12-15). Stage-optimal ≈70.6, the same as fringe. Many top players switch per scramble.thread; computed budgets
    Row vs column choice−1.9 moves on 3x3
    At every reduction, solve whichever of the row or column is cheaper. Measured on the 3x3: 25.8 → 23.9.computed
    Endpoint-aware stages−1.4 moves on 3x3
    Among equally short ways to finish a stage, pick the one that leaves the next stage easiest (gap position, pairs pre-formed). Measured: 27.3 → 25.8.computed
    Last-two / corner trick
    Last two tiles of a row: park the last tile in the second-last cell with the second-last tile under it, rotate in. Mirror for columns. This is the baseline. Every case where a direct pair insertion is shorter is a saved move.thread; many tutorials
    Pairs and blocks
    Solve 1-2, 3-4, 9-13, 7-8, 10-14 as dominoes. Tiles that are already adjacent in solved order are a free head start.thread (LNZ's block method)
    Line up and send
    Put several fringe tiles in one column in the right order, then move them all up in one looping motion.thread
    Hook for 10/14
    Get 14 beside 9 (10 not under it), bring 10 to the top, set it next to 14, put the gap under 14, drop 14 so 10 follows. This leaves a pure 3-cycle.thread (Jaycee)
    Force the last 3
    While solving 9 and 13, or 6-7-8, steer the remaining tiles so the final 2x2 is a short rotation. The 2x2 averages 3 moves but can take 6.thread (Ben)
    Parking a tile out of place
    Leave a tile in a "wrong" cell that the rest of the solve fixes for free. Example: Ranzha's row-first variant puts 12 in the 16 slot early and pushes it up at the end.thread
    Cross-stage planningneeded for sub-70
    Plan the row and column of an L together, and carry next-stage tiles along. Stage-perfect play is ≈70, so any consistent result under that requires it. Slow movecount ao100 records reach 55.9.computed; leaderboard moves PBs
    Pre-planning
    Plan the start before the timer runs. Most sims start the clock on your first move. Check yours.

    Execution

    Hover controlsthe standard
    Mouse hover: the gap follows the pointer. The README calls it the most common input for speedsolving and calls keyboard objectively slower. Click mode exists mainly for FMC.Slidysim README
    Zero animation
    Set move duration to 0 in hover mode, and avoid tile gaps in hover mode.README
    Lookahead
    Find the next tile while finishing the current one, so the first move of each stage comes at full speed. In the data, the difference between rank 100 and rank 10 is mostly TPS: 13.7 → 24.9.leaderboard bands
    Bursts on known endings
    2x2 cycles and 3x2 solutions are short enough to run at your peak TPS. TPS PBs: 30.8 (ao100), 34.1 (ao12), 43.8 (single).leaderboard TPS PBs
    Never-stop solving
    Hold a TPS low enough that you never pause, then raise it. Pauses cost more than slow moves.
    Display-type training
    Slidysim display types change what you see. Minimal, Row minimal and Fringe minimal hide most tiles; Fading tiles and Last move test memory and tracking; Vanish on solved hides finished tiles; Manhattan and Vectors show distance or direction to home. Practice lookahead in them, then go back to Standard.README
    3x3 as TPS training
    The 3x3 is mostly execution: top ao100s run 25–37 TPS. Use it to warm up.
    Marathons and relays
    x10 and x42 marathons, and 2-N, width and height relays, build endurance and count in the power rankings.README; leaderboard categories
    BLD
    Numbers vanish after the first move. This is an extreme pre-planning drill; a 4.560 single is recorded.README; Speedsolving wiki
    Color scheme
    "Fringe 3" with Rainbow colors for 4x4. Grids (row grids, stacked layers) for big puzzles, switched with keys 1/2 mid-solve.README
    Retry a scramble
    Ctrl+Z replays the previous scramble without counting it. Use it to re-solve a bad solve with a better plan.README

    Setup and tools

    Slidysim

    • Controls: Hover. Move duration 0. Scrambler: Random permutation (the only one accepted for records).
    • Keyboard+macros, and save/load used to replay a solution, are not allowed on the leaderboard.
    • Hold Ctrl or a mouse button to block moves in hover mode while you position the cursor before a solve.
    • Run the Records Manager beside Slidysim to upload PBs to the Open Slidysim leaderboard.

    Analysis

    • Open Slidysim: rankings, replays with per-move timing, MD and Moves/MD, and an optimal solver for replays.
    • Speedsliding Discord bot: !solve, !getwr, !rank, !draw, !animate.
    • Slidysim Session Tracker (SST) for long-term stats.
    • Rankings use Power tiers (below) and Kinch scores across categories.
    Community standard

    Open Slidysim tiers

    These are the thresholds the leaderboard itself uses for ranks. You reach a tier when your average is at or under the listed time. FMC is the untimed fewest-moves ranking. Its thresholds show what each level can do with unlimited thinking time, so don't expect them at speed.
